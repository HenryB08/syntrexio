PART I — THE COMPANY
1. WHAT SYNTREX IS

Syntrex is the AI infrastructure layer behind operating companies.

We run a business's entire digital and AI back end. Presence, visibility, conversion, and operations. One partner, one system, one point of accountability.

The line: Nobody owns the outcome. We do.

2. THE PROBLEM WE EXIST FOR

Companies are spending real money on AI and getting nothing.

95% of enterprise AI pilots produce no measurable P&L impact, despite $30 to $40 billion spent. (MIT Project NANDA, State of AI in Business, July 2025. 150 interviews, 350 employee surveys, 300 deployments.)
42% of companies scrapped most of their AI initiatives in 2025, up from 17% in 2024. The average company abandoned 46% of its AI proofs of concept. (S&P Global Market Intelligence, 1,000+ respondents, June 2025.)
40%+ of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027 due to unclear value and weak controls. (Gartner, June 2025.)
Roughly 70% of AI failure is people and process. Only 20% is technology. (RAND Corporation, 2024.)

The failure is not the technology. It is that AI gets bought in fragments, embedded in nothing, and nobody is accountable for the result.

The correction: one system that knows the whole business, one partner who owns the whole outcome, and proof every month that it worked.

PART II — HOW WE DELIVER
4. THE DELIVERY ARCHITECTURE

Traditional agency: headcount produces the work. Every new client requires more people. Revenue per employee runs $163,000 to $220,000 and net margins land at 8 to 19%. (Promethean Research, 2026 State of Digital Services, 119 agency leaders.)

Syntrex: an orchestrated agent fleet produces the work. Humans direct, review, and own the outcome.

Layer 1 — The Client Encoding. Every client is encoded once: brand voice, visual system, products, pricing, policies, competitors, goals, constraints. Persistent, stored in shared memory. Every output passes through it.

Layer 2 — The Fleet. Parallel agents in isolated environments, one per workstream. Websites, content, search, automations, reporting, and outreach run concurrently rather than sequentially. A build that takes an agency six weeks takes Syntrex days.

Layer 3 — Always-On. Agents run on dedicated infrastructure whether or not anyone is at a laptop. Reports generate on schedule. Monitoring alerts before the client notices. Follow-ups fire automatically.

Layer 4 — Human Judgment. A human reviews and approves everything client-facing before it ships. Strategy, taste, escalation, and accountability stay human permanently. This is not a limitation. It is the product.

Compounding. Every client encoded makes the system faster at the next one. Every approval, rejection, and edit is stored as structured preference data. Month six needs fewer corrections than month one, permanently. Human labor does not compound. This does.

5. THE ONE THING THAT MUST STAY TRUE

The entire model rests on one condition: the fleet must reliably deliver roughly 80% of guaranteed outcomes with systematized human oversight, across at least three to five concurrent clients, sustained over six months.

If that holds, everything in this document works. If it does not, margins collapse to a normal agency, the guarantee becomes a liability, and the business cannot scale past the founder.

Prove this before scaling past five clients. It is the only benchmark that matters in year one.

PART IV — PRICING
7. THE PRICING RULE

Every price is derived from projected human hours multiplied by a $350 target hourly value, then checked against market rate to confirm it lands meaningfully below agency pricing without competing on price.

Agent compute is a rounding error. Human review time is the real cost. When a new service is added, price it with this rule. Do not invent numbers.

Because the numbers come from a rule, they do not change when the mood changes.

PART V — THE PROOF LAYER
8. THE RECEIPT

Every retainer client receives a monthly Receipt. One document showing what the system did and what it was worth. Leads captured, hours returned, citations gained, rankings moved, content shipped, automations run, tasks completed.

Receipts are immutable once issued. A later change in assumptions never alters a past Receipt.

Why this exists: the top three reasons clients leave are lack of proactive strategic guidance (68%), poor communication (57%), and inability to demonstrate value (53%). Price ranks sixth. The Receipt attacks the top three automatically, every month.

9. THE GUARANTEE

We guarantee only what we control. The remedy is our labor, never a refund.

This is the structure real firms use, and it is the only version that is both credible and survivable.

What is guaranteed
Service    Guaranteed metric
AI search optimization    Citation presence for agreed target queries
Traditional SEO    Ranking movement for agreed target terms
Content    Volume and publication schedule delivered
Automation    Hours removed from an agreed workflow
Agent Workforce    Task volume completed per period
Presence builds    Delivery against agreed spec and timeline
What is never guaranteed

Revenue. Closed deals. Conversion rate. Anything dependent on the client's sales team, pricing, product, or market. We do not control these and will not pretend otherwise.

The mechanics
One metric per engagement. Named in writing.
A documented baseline captured before work begins.
A defined window, typically 90 days.
A single source of truth for measurement, agreed at signing.
If the metric is not hit, we keep working at no additional cost until it is.
What the client provides

The labor remedy assumes the client keeps the work moving, so the guarantee carries four conditions. Each is measured by a date, not by judgment.

Approval turnaround: submitted work is approved, or returned with specific requested changes, within five business days of delivery.
Access: the systems and accounts named at kickoff are granted within five business days of the request.
Information: facts only the client holds, and that we cannot obtain ourselves, are provided within five business days of the request.
Point of contact: one named person with authority to approve, reachable through the channel agreed at kickoff.

When a condition is missed, the guarantee window pauses on the dated miss and resumes on the date the item is resolved. A pause never voids the guarantee, it only stops the clock. Every pause is recorded on that month's Receipt with the date it began and the date it ended, so the count is visible to both sides. Nothing here turns on our opinion of the client, only on dated events either side can verify.

Why this is safe

The remedy is time, and for an agent-leveraged operation time is cheap. There is no cash exposure. The qualification is the protection: only offer the guarantee to clients whose situation you have diagnosed and whose baseline you have measured. The diagnostic exists partly for this reason.

Roughly 99% of clients will not abuse a guarantee if qualification is done properly.

PART VI — HOW WE SELL
10. THE SALES PROCESS

Channel: warm referral and partner introduction only. Cold outreach has produced essentially nothing and is not part of the model. 92% of B2B buyers begin with at least one vendor in mind and 41% with a single preferred vendor. Buying is confirmation, not selection. Be the vendor already in mind.

First conversation. Discovery, not pitching. Ask 11 to 14 questions. Listen 80% of the time. Discuss price on the first call. More than half of prospects want it; only 23% of sellers do it.

Qualify hard. Who is the economic buyer. What is the decision process. What is the measurable pain. What does success look like in numbers. Is there a champion inside.

Sell the diagnostic, not the engagement. The next step is always the paid diagnostic. This converts a sales conversation into a paid engagement immediately.

Proposal. Sent within 24 hours. Proposals sent within a day close at 50%+ higher rates than those sent after 72 hours. Three options, never one price. Tiered proposals win roughly 18% more often, and bundled pricing is 36% more likely to be approved than standard pricing. Open with the answer, not a company bio. Review it live before asking for a decision. Close rates double when multiple stakeholders view it.

Follow up relentlessly. 80% of won deals require 5 to 12 follow-ups. Only 2% close on first contact.

Objections. Only 30% of lost deals are actually lost on price, though sellers believe it is 70%. When you hear price, look for unclear value or unclear decision process first.

11. THE BURNED BUYER PLAYBOOK

For prospects who have already lost money on AI. This is the most common and most winnable situation in the market right now.

Open with their story. "Walk me through what you tried, what you were promised, and where it broke down."
Diagnose the failure. Was there a defined success metric? A baseline? Who owned the outcome? What was delivered against what was promised? These questions surface the real answer, which is almost always that nobody owned the outcome.
Never disparage the prior vendor. Explain the structural difference instead: one metric, a baseline, milestone payments, and we work free until it hits.
De-risk with the diagnostic. Small, paid, credited, defined deliverable.
Prove with peers. HALT Fire's numbers, delivered by the same system.

82% of B2B buyers prioritize credibility over likability. Do not try to be liked. Be verifiable.

PART VII — HOW WE KEEP AND GROW CLIENTS
13. ONBOARDING

43% of B2B churn happens in the first 90 days. (Moxo, 2026 State of Churn.) 23% of churn is attributable to onboarding alone. (Customer Success Association.) A client who completes intake within 48 hours retains above 85%. One who drags past ten days retains below 50%. (OnboardMap.)

Onboarding speed predicts retention better than early results do.

The standard:

Within 24 hours: written kickoff summary, single hub for all access and credentials, recorded walkthrough, named contact, mutual 30/60/90 day goals with owners and dates.
Within 7 days: something real and working shipped. Not a plan. Not a discovery deck.
Written scope at kickoff: success metrics with numbers, what is in and out of scope, how changes are handled, response time expectations.
Monthly: the Receipt plus a 30-minute call. Agencies doing both renew better than those sending reports alone.
Quarterly: business review, 60% of the agenda on the next 90 days.

62% of agency leaders say delays getting system access damage client confidence. Intake speed is the highest-leverage retention move available.

Targets: annual churn below 20%. Top performers hold 8 to 10%. Above 30% means stop selling and fix delivery.

14. EXPANSION

Selling to an existing client succeeds 60 to 70% of the time. Selling to a new prospect succeeds 5 to 20%. (Farris et al., Marketing Metrics.) Expansion revenue costs roughly five times less than new acquisition.

The sequence: Diagnostic -> Visibility retainer -> add Conversion once traffic flows -> add Operations -> land Agent Workforce -> Full Stack.

Visibility is the entry point because GEO has the strongest growth and pricing power and the lowest tooling cost. Never lead with a website.

Timing: pitch expansion immediately after an ROI milestone. Expansion conversations following a milestone are 3.5 times more likely to succeed. Never on your own calendar.

Raising prices: 60 days notice, anchored to value. Set new rates for new clients first, let close rates settle over 15 to 20 proposals, then move existing accounts.

Target: net revenue retention above 100%, trending to 120%. Below 100% means the business leaks faster than it fills, regardless of new logos.

Companies running structured quarterly reviews see 24% higher retention and 18% higher net revenue retention. 35 to 50% of expansion bookings originate in those reviews. (Gainsight.)

15. CHANNELS

Referral program. Refer someone. If they become a paying client, earn 10% of their first project or first three months of retainer, as cash or credit toward your own work. Credit stacks; enough referrals covers a referrer's own build entirely. Paid once the referred client has onboarded and paid. Ask at the moment of a visible win, never at the end of an engagement.

Keep total cost of sales under 20% of revenue. Above that, margin erodes.

White label and subcontracting. Other agencies resell Syntrex builds under their own brand at their markup. They keep the client relationship, Syntrex owns delivery. This is the highest-leverage channel because it scales revenue with no outbound effort and no headcount. Kinetix is the template. Target three more.

Advisor alliances. Accountants, IT and MSP firms, consultants, business brokers. They sit closest to the problem and refer earliest. Simple written referral agreements. For reference, ADP pays accountants up to 75% of referral fees over 12 months, which shows how valuable these relationships are considered.

Complementary partners. Design firms, PR shops, fractional executives serving the same buyers.

Lower priority: speaking, events, associations, community. Real but slow for a small operator.

PART VIII — OPERATIONS
16. SYSTEMATIZING DELIVERY

The greatest risk to this business is founder dependence. Every hour spent systematizing buys back permanent capacity.

Build SOPs by recording delivery. Run an engagement two or three times while narrating decisions, then extract into checklists and templates.

Document the standard, not just the steps. Every SOP ends with a definition of done and a QA checklist, so quality does not depend on who or what produced the work.

Encoding hierarchy: training becomes documentation, documentation becomes checklists, checklists become templates, templates become automation. Push every lesson as far toward automation as it will go.

Client communication systems reduce founder involvement more than any other single change. One hub, templated reporting, scheduled cadences.

Clean handoffs. Define exactly what must be true before work moves between a person and an agent, and who checks it against the standard. This is what makes adding a second person possible without retraining everything.

17. NON-NEGOTIABLES

Contracts. The client owns their deliverables and their data. Syntrex owns the underlying system, templates, encodings, and tooling. Never work-for-hire on the system itself. This one clause is the difference between building an asset and renting out labor.

Human approval. Nothing client-facing ships without human review, at any scale.

Client isolation. No client's context, data, or encoding ever touches another's.

Honest claims. Capability language can be ambitious. Client counts, revenue, team size, and results must survive due diligence.

No outsourcing language. Everything is built and led in-house. Never signal a vetted network, offshore partners, or sourced specialists.

Published pricing. We do not hide numbers.

Quality over volume. Never take a client we cannot deliver for. One bad outcome travels further than five good ones in a referral business.

19. THE STAGES

Stage 1 — Prove the system (0 to 6 clients). Every process documented as it is built. The output is not revenue, it is the template. Prove the fleet holds across three to five concurrent clients without constant firefighting. Convert HALT Fire and Doughbrik's Wavers into numbers-first case studies. Land SupplyCaddy through the burned-buyer playbook and treat that relationship as a channel, not just an account. Target: $30,000 monthly recurring.

Stage 2 — Leverage (6 to 15 clients). Templates cut delivery time by two thirds. Second person on review and client communication. Formalize white label with three partner agencies. Agent Workforce becomes the flagship offer. Quarterly reviews on every account. Target: $70,000 to $100,000 monthly recurring.

Stage 3 — Infrastructure (15 to 40 clients). Always-on fleet at full scale. Team of three to five on review and relationships, never on production. Advisor referral network producing steady inbound. By now the same system has been built 20+ times, which reveals the repeatable product. Target: $200,000+ monthly recurring, $2.5M+ annually.

Stage 4 — The product. The internal delivery system, packaged so a company can run its own agent workforce on subscription, sold to companies that are not Syntrex clients. Services fund the build. This is where the business stops being capped by hours.

Stage 5 — Platform exceeds services. When platform revenue passes service revenue, the valuation logic changes entirely. A services business sells for 3 to 5 times profit. A software business sells for 4 to 5 times revenue with far higher margins beneath it. That gap is the entire reason the product exists.

20. THE HONEST CONDITION

An agency alone, even an excellent one, reaches a few million a year and sells for a few times profit. That is a very good outcome and it is not the outcome this document is aimed at.

The large outcome requires the product. Mailchimp began as a web design agency, shut the agency down to focus on the product, and sold for roughly $12 billion. That is the shape of the path, and it is not automatic.

The rule that protects it: every client engagement is built as a configuration of one shared system, never as a one-off. The moment a build is bespoke, that work evaporates instead of compounding. This decision gets made on the first client, not the twentieth.

The signal to watch for: when the same work has been delivered for several clients with the same process, that is the product. It will not feel exciting. The first thing worth productizing is usually the one that bores you.
