## HOW TO USE THIS (internal, delete before sending)

**Who this is for.** A prospect who has already spent money on AI, marketing automation, or an agency engagement and got nothing measurable back. This is the most common and most winnable situation in the market right now. It is also the one where a normal proposal fails, because a normal proposal reads exactly like the one that burned them.

**The one thing that makes this version different.** A standard proposal opens with the recommendation. This one opens with the recommendation *and* the structural reason it will not fail the way the last one did. They are not buying a service. They are buying a structure that makes the previous failure impossible to repeat. Sell the structure.

**Never disparage the prior vendor.** Not one line. Not implied. The moment you attack the last provider you become a salesperson attacking a competitor, and they have heard that pitch before, from the vendor that failed them. Diagnose the structure instead. The answer is almost always that nobody owned the outcome, and that is a structural fact, not an insult.

**Do the diagnosis on the call, not in the document.** Before this proposal exists you should have asked, on a call, in this order:

1. "Walk me through what you tried, what you were promised, and where it broke down."
2. "Was there a defined success metric?"
3. "Was there a baseline measured before it started?"
4. "Who owned the outcome?"
5. "What was actually delivered against what was promised?"

Their answers go into Section 1 of this document in their own words. If you cannot fill Section 1 with direct quotes, you have not earned the right to send this yet. Go back and have the call.

**Credibility over likability.** B2B buyers prioritize credibility over likability, and a burned buyer prioritizes it more than anyone. Do not try to be liked. Be verifiable. Every claim in this document should be one they can check.

**De-risk with the diagnostic.** If they have not yet paid for the AI Systems Diagnostic, the answer to a burned buyer is almost never a large engagement. It is the $3,500 diagnostic: small, paid, credited, with a defined deliverable. This template is for the engagement that follows it. If you are still upstream of that, sell the diagnostic and stop.

**Still three options, never one price.** Same rule. But weight them lower than you would for a fresh buyer. A burned buyer earns trust in increments. Recommending the largest option to someone who just lost money reads as the same mistake in a new suit.

**Customize every time:** Section 1 quotes, Section 2 comparison table, the guaranteed metric with baseline and window, which option is Recommended, the week one deliverable, and the timeline dates.

**Never do:** invent a price, invent a statistic, name a client other than HALT Fire, Doughbrik's Wavers, or Kinetix, claim headcount, guarantee revenue or closed deals, name the prior vendor in writing, or offer a payment plan as a concession after a price objection.

## 9. What happens in week one

**Hour 0 to 24:** written kickoff summary with scope, success metrics, what is in and out, how changes get handled, response times, and your named contact. A single hub for all access and credentials. A recorded walkthrough. Mutual 30/60/90 day goals with an owner and a date on every line.

**Hour 24 to 48:** intake complete and the baseline for {{GUARANTEED_METRIC}} measured and locked. This is the one part that needs you. Clients who finish intake inside 48 hours consistently do better than clients who take ten days, and the difference is large enough that we treat intake speed as the single highest-leverage thing you do.

**Day 2 to 7:** your business gets encoded once, brand voice through constraints, and every output from then on passes through it. {{WEEK_ONE_DELIVERABLE}} gets built, reviewed by a human, and shipped.

**Day 7:** {{WEEK_ONE_DELIVERABLE}} is live.

**Every month:** the Receipt. One page, what the system did and what it was worth, measured against the baseline. Receipts are immutable once issued, so a later change in assumptions never quietly rewrites a past month in our favor. Plus a 30 minute call.

**Every quarter:** business review, with 60% of the agenda on the next 90 days.

## PRICING REFERENCE (internal, delete before sending)

Identical to the standard proposal template. See `operations/proposal-template.md`, section titled PRICING REFERENCE, for retainers, builds, guaranteed metrics by service, and the three-option shapes. Never quote below those numbers, and never discount to win a burned buyer back. A discount confirms their suspicion that the price was arbitrary the first time.

**One burned-buyer-specific pricing note:** the pressure to shrink the price is strongest here and it is the wrong lever. Shrink the *scope* instead. A smaller engagement at full rate rebuilds trust. A full engagement at a cut rate teaches them that the number was never real.
