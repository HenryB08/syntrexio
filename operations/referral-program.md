## Why this is the primary channel

Warm referral and partner introduction is the channel. Cold outreach has produced essentially nothing and is not part of the model.

84% of B2B sales begin with a referral, yet only 30% of agencies have a formal referral program. 92% of B2B buyers begin their process with at least one vendor already in mind and 41% with a single preferred vendor, which means buying is confirmation rather than selection. The entire job of this program is to be the vendor already in mind when that process starts.

The constraint that governs it: **keep total cost of sales under 20% of revenue.** A 10% referral fee sits comfortably inside that with room for everything else. Anything above 20% erodes margin faster than the volume replaces it.

## The terms

**The offer.** Refer someone. If they become a paying client, earn 10% of their first project or their first three months of retainer.

**Paid as cash or as credit** toward the referrer's own work, at the referrer's choice.

**Credit stacks.** Enough referrals covers a referrer's own build entirely. This is the version most referrers choose and it is the better outcome for both sides: it costs us delivery capacity rather than cash, and it deepens their engagement instead of ending the transaction.

**Paid once the referred client has onboarded and paid.** Not on signature, not on first contact. Onboarded and paid.

**Who can refer.** Anyone. Current clients, past clients, partners, advisors, people who have never bought anything from us. There is no membership step and no application.

**No cap.** No limit on the number of referrals or the total earned.

**Ask at the moment of a visible win. Never at the end of an engagement.** This is the timing rule and it is the part most programs get wrong. See the timing section below.

## The rules

**Attribution.** The referrer is whoever introduced us first, recorded at the time of the introduction. Not whoever followed up most recently.

**Attribution window: 12 months** from the introduction. If a referred prospect signs 11 months later, the fee is owed. At 13 months it is not, though pay it anyway if the referrer clearly caused it. The window exists to prevent disputes, not to avoid paying people.

**No self-referral.** A client cannot refer their own second entity and collect a fee on it. That is what portfolio pricing is for: 10% off the second brand, 15% off everything beyond, tied to committed volume.

**One referrer per client.** If two people introduce the same prospect, the first recorded introduction takes it. Tell the second person, immediately and directly, rather than letting them find out when no payment arrives.

**Referral fees are never stacked with a discount** on the same engagement. A referred client gets the published price. If someone asks for both, the fee is the thing that survives, because the referrer is the reason the deal exists.

**Not paid on renewals or expansions.** First project or first three months of retainer, once. A referrer who wants ongoing economics is describing a white label or partner relationship, which is a different and better arrangement. Route them there.

**Quality over volume applies here too.** Never take a referred client we cannot deliver for, even when refusing costs a relationship. One bad outcome travels further than five good ones in a referral business, and a bad outcome delivered to a referrer's contact damages two relationships at once.

**Nothing is fabricated to earn a referral.** No invented results, no client counts, no names beyond HALT Fire, Doughbrik's Wavers, and Kinetix. The referrer is putting their own credibility behind us and the fastest way to lose the channel permanently is to hand them something that does not survive a phone call.

## Timing: when to ask

**The rule: ask at the moment of a visible win. Never at the end of an engagement.**

The end of an engagement is the worst possible moment and it is the moment most agencies choose. The client is disengaging, the last thing that happened is an ending, and the ask reads as a final transaction. At a visible win the client is actively pleased, has just been reminded of the value, and has a specific thing they can describe to someone else.

### The moments that qualify

| Moment | Why it works |
|---|---|
| The guaranteed metric is hit, at the QBR | The strongest one. They have a number in front of them |
| A Receipt lands showing a significant jump | Ask on the review call, in the same breath as the number |
| The day 7 deliverable ships and lands well | Early, and surprisingly effective, because the surprise is fresh |
| The client says something unprompted and positive | Take it as an invitation. It usually is one |
| The client shares our work internally or publicly | They are already referring. Formalize it |
| An expansion is agreed | They have just voted with their budget |
| An automation crosses a milestone they care about | "That is 200 hours back this year" is a referable sentence |

### The moments that do not qualify

- The end of an engagement
- Immediately after a missed guarantee, a slipped date, or a service failure
- The same conversation as a price increase
- During a renewal negotiation
- More than once a quarter to the same person

### How often

Once a quarter per relationship, at most, and only when a qualifying moment exists. If no qualifying moment occurred this quarter, do not ask. An ask with no win behind it teaches the person that the ask is on a schedule, and after that they discount it.

## The exact language

Use these. They are short on purpose. A long referral ask reads as a favor being requested, which is the wrong frame: this is a paid arrangement being offered.

### At a QBR where the metric was hit

> "Before we wrap. You said at kickoff you wanted {{METRIC}} at {{TARGET}} and we are at {{CURRENT}}. If anyone in your network is dealing with what you were dealing with in {{MONTH}}, send them my way. If they become a client you get 10% of their first three months, cash or credit against your own work. No obligation and I will not chase you about it."

### On a monthly call, after a strong Receipt

> "That number is the kind of thing people ask about. If it comes up, I would rather they hear it from you than from our website. And if you send someone who becomes a client, there is 10% in it for you, cash or credit."

### When the client says something positive unprompted

> "That is good to hear. Genuinely, the most useful thing you could do with that is tell one person. We pay 10% of a first project or first three months if it turns into a client, but the introduction is worth more to me than what it costs."

### To a partner or agency who could resell

> "You could refer these and take 10%. Or you could white label it: you keep the client relationship and the markup, we own delivery and stay invisible. The second one is worth considerably more to you. Which do you want to talk about?"

*Always steer an agency toward white label. Kinetix is the template. A white label partner is worth more than a referrer by an order of magnitude, because it scales revenue with no outbound effort and no headcount, and the Foundation target is three more of them.*

### To an advisor, accountant, MSP, or consultant

> "You see these problems before anyone else does. If you send someone my way and they become a client, it is 10% of their first project or first three months. Some people take it as cash, most take it as credit against their own work. I will put it in writing so it is not a handshake."

*Advisor alliances sit closest to the problem and refer earliest. Always follow with a simple written agreement, because these relationships pay out over years and a handshake does not survive their internal compliance review.*

### After a referral is paid

> "That one closed. Your {{AMOUNT}} is on the way as {{CASH_OR_CREDIT}}. Thank you, and if there is anyone else, same deal."

*Close the loop every single time, even when the referral did not convert. Especially then.*

### When a referral does not convert

> "Wanted to close the loop on {{NAME}}. It did not go ahead, {{REASON}}. No reflection on the introduction, which was a good fit. Thanks for making it."

*A referrer who is told what happened refers again. A referrer who hears nothing assumes the introduction was wasted and does not.*

## Tracking

Referrals live in the CRM, not in a spreadsheet and not in memory. Every referral gets a record at the moment of introduction, before anyone knows whether it will convert.

### What is recorded

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| Referrer name and contact | |
| Referrer relationship | Client, past client, partner, advisor, other |
| Referred company and contact | |
| Introduction date | Starts the 12 month attribution window |
| Source | Referral page form, direct message, verbal, partner |
| Status | Introduced, contacted, diagnostic sold, proposal sent, closed won, closed lost |
| Engagement value | Basis for the fee |
| Fee owed | 10% of first project or first three months |
| Payout preference | Cash or credit |
| Payout status | Owed, paid, credited, not applicable |
| Payout date | |
| Loop closed with referrer | Yes or no. This field is not optional |

### The cadence

| When | What happens |
|---|---|
| Introduction received | Record created same day. Referrer thanked within 24 hours |
| Within 48 hours | Referred prospect contacted. A referral that sits is a referrer who does not refer again |
| Weekly | Open referrals reviewed alongside the pipeline |
| On close won | Fee calculated, payout preference confirmed, payout scheduled |
| Client onboarded and paid | **Fee paid within 15 days.** This is the trigger, and it is not flexible |
| On close lost | Referrer told, with the reason, within 5 days |
| Monthly | Cost of sales checked. Referral fees plus every other acquisition cost stays under 20% of revenue |
| Quarterly | Program reviewed: referrals received, conversion rate, fees paid, and who is referring |

### Credit accounting

Credit is tracked as a balance against the referrer's account and drawn down against invoices.

- Credit never expires
- Credit applies to any Syntrex work: retainers, builds, or the diagnostic
- Credit is not transferable and is not convertible back to cash once elected
- Credit balance appears on every invoice, so it is visible without asking
- If a referrer's credit balance exceeds their engagement value, the excess carries forward. It is not paid out in cash

### What gets reviewed quarterly

- Referrals received, by source and by referrer
- Conversion rate from introduction to closed won
- Total fees paid, cash versus credit
- Total cost of sales as a percentage of revenue, against the 20% ceiling
- Referrers who have referred more than once. These are the relationships worth investing in directly
- Referrers who were asked and never referred. Usually a signal the ask was mistimed rather than that the relationship is weak
- Any referral where the loop was not closed. Fix immediately

## Escalating a referrer into a partner

Referral is the entry point. It is not the ceiling, and the highest-value relationships all start here.

| Signal | Escalate to |
|---|---|
| Refers twice in two quarters | Written referral agreement, and a direct conversation about what they see in their own network |
| Is an agency or services firm with clients of their own | **White label.** They keep the client relationship and their markup, Syntrex owns delivery. Highest-leverage channel available, because it scales revenue with no outbound effort and no headcount. Kinetix is the template and the target is three more |
| Is an accountant, MSP, IT firm, consultant, or business broker | **Advisor alliance,** with a simple written agreement. They sit closest to the problem and refer earliest. ADP pays accountants up to 75% of referral fees over 12 months, which is a useful indication of what these relationships are worth to companies that have measured them |
| Is a design firm, PR shop, or fractional executive serving the same buyers | **Complementary partnership.** Reciprocal referral, no fee in either direction, or fee both ways. Agree which, in writing, at the start |

The escalation conversation is worth having early. A partner who has been referring for a year at 10% will reasonably ask why nobody offered them the better arrangement sooner.
